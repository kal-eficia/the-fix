01

The short version

Updated 28 Nov: includes Gibbs-White's two goals in Forest's 3-2 win over Chelsea.

Pochettino is now United's manager, Bruno's future is up in the air, and both of these have been linked with Old Trafford. They're very different bets. We compared each with every Premier League attacking midfielder over three seasons, with penalties stripped out.

Palmer

The best numbers of any 10 in the league over three seasons, even without the penalties. It wasn't a one-season wonder, and Pochettino has already got the best out of him once. The worry is the last 18 months.

Gibbs-White

Always available, Forest's captain, and he does it with much less of the ball. But he's been a creator, then a scorer, then a creator again, and never top-quarter at both in the same season.

Neither of them makes chances like Bruno. That part doesn't get replaced like for like.

02

The bar is Bruno

Start with the job. Bruno's been 1st or 2nd among Premier League attacking midfielders for chances created in every season we looked at. Switch the stat and the season and see where the two options land.

03

Only one has done both

A 10 has two jobs: get on the end of chances and make them for others. Across is non-penalty xG per 90 (chances he gets), up is xA per 90 (chances he makes). Every dot is one player's season.

Palmer's two big seasons are 1st and 3rd of the 48 for the two added together. Bruno's seasons are 6th, 10th and 12th, and almost all of that is the making. Gibbs-White's best is 18th.

04

The Poch season

2023/24 at Chelsea: 22 league goals and 11 assists, more goal involvements than anyone in the league, in his first season as a regular. The obvious question is how much of that was penalties.

Nine of the 22, and he scored every one. Take them all out and it's still the best attacking-midfielder season in the league in three years: 1st of 48 for non-penalty xG and xA added together.

One of his best skills is his mentality and his capacity to deal with the pressure.Pochettino, April 2024, via premierleague.com
05

Not a one-off

The season after Pochettino left, Palmer actually got better chances: 13.4 non-penalty xG against 11.1. He scored fewer because he took fewer penalties and finished a bit colder. Goals minus xG swings from year to year; the chances are the steadier signal. Two elite seasons in a row passes our two-season test.

The worry is what came after: a groin problem that kept him out for much of autumn 2025, 1,965 league minutes last season, and middling numbers in 1,060 minutes this season. That's a small sample, so we wouldn't read much into it either way.

06

Three different Gibbs-Whites

Gibbs-White's profile keeps changing. In 2023/24 he was a creator (10 assists). In 2025/26 he became a goalscorer. This season he's back to making chances, and he's 1st of 14 for big chances created.

Last season's 14 open-play goals came from 10.3 xG. Beating your xG by nearly four is a hot streak, and those rarely repeat: this season it's one from 2.2. That one came on Friday night, when he scored twice (the other a penalty) in Forest's 3-2 win over Palmer's Chelsea, with the top FotMob rating on the pitch (8.9). He's never been top-quarter for scoring and creating in the same season.

There is real context, though. Forest had 40.3%, 40.8% and 46.8% of the ball across these three seasons. Chelsea had 59.0%, 57.2% and 57.7%. Gibbs-White's numbers come with a lot less possession.

07

Where they shoot from

Every non-penalty shot, bigger circle for a better chance. Pick a player and a season.

Palmer shoots a lot from distance: more than half his non-penalty shots in both big seasons came from outside the box, almost all with his left foot. Gibbs-White's scoring season came when he moved his shooting closer: 34% from outside the box, down from 51% two seasons before.

08

The catch for each

League minutes every season. One is always out there.

Palmer: the injury is the obvious one. The other is the work without the ball. In his Poch season he was last of 14 attacking midfielders for tackles and for recoveries per 90. Chelsea had the ball 59% of the time, which keeps those numbers down, but he's never going to be a 10 who presses for fun.

Gibbs-White: you're buying reliability and graft, not Palmer's ceiling. He was 18th of 19 for tackles last season too, so neither is a Bruno-style runner.

09

So who do you take?

Palmer

The swing for the fences. Highest ceiling of any 10 in the league, a manager who's already unlocked him, and Chelsea have him tied down to 2034, so he won't come cheap.

Gibbs-White

The safer bet. Plays every week, captains his club, contract to 2028. Gives you a good 10, not a great one.

Method and sources

  • Data: FotMob (Opta) season stats and shot maps for Palmer and Gibbs-White, and FotMob's Premier League stats for every attacking midfielder, pulled 28 Nov 2026, so Forest's 3-2 win over Chelsea on 27 Nov is included.
  • Comparison set: players whose main FotMob position is attacking midfielder, min 1,350 minutes a season (Opta's radar threshold): 14 in 2023/24, 15 in 2024/25, 19 in 2025/26. That's 48 player-seasons. For 2026/27 so far, min 500 minutes (14 players).
  • Penalties out: non-penalty xG and goals are counted from the shot maps. Chances created and xA include set pieces.
  • Two-season rule: something only counts as a trait if it shows up in two seasons. Goals minus xG is treated as mostly noise.
  • Context: team possession from FotMob's Premier League team stats. Tackles and recoveries depend on how much a team is without the ball.
  • News: Pochettino's appointment (The Analyst); United linked with Gibbs-White as a Bruno replacement (GOAL); Palmer's 2025 groin injury (SI). Contracts from FotMob.
  • What we didn't have: tracking data and possession-value models (OBV, xT). The halftone photos are printed from press images.
