How Man United won the ball back at Sporting

They won it in better places than all season. Then they stopped.

01

The short version

United won the ball back about as often as Sporting (63 times to 66), but far higher up the pitch: 24 of their regains came in the final 40% of the pitch, against Sporting's 5. They pressed hardest before half-time, won the ball highest in the 15 minutes after the break, and never won it in Sporting's half again after the 67th minute. The gap was the end product: those 24 high regains led to two shots.

02

Where United won it

United won the ball most often around halfway and in Sporting's half, and spread it evenly across the width: 23 on the right, 19 in the middle, 21 on the left. Sporting were the opposite: 37 of their 66 regains came in their own third, and only 3 in United's.

  • The front of the press won it highest. Amad won it in the final 40% six times, more than anyone. Mainoo and Tielemans did it four times each, Fernandes twice.
  • The back line stepped in too. Maguire won it high three times by stepping out on the left: twice in first-half stoppage time and once at 67 minutes.
  • The deep regains clustered on the flanks. 20 of United's 28 own-half regains came out wide, the channels Sporting's wing-backs worked in.
03

When United won it

The press ran from the 15th minute to roughly the 67th, in two waves either side of half-time, and then stopped.

  • First half: the hardest press. Sporting managed about 10 passes in their own build-up for every United challenge, against about 16 after the break. United also had far more of the ball up the pitch: 141 passes in Sporting's half before the break, 71 after it (FotMob).
  • 45 to 67 minutes: the highest regains. Sporting had more of the ball now, so United pressed in bursts rather than all the time, and those bursts won it close to goal. Mainoo won it three times between the 51st and 55th minutes, and the two goals came in this spell (48th and 50th minutes).
  • After 67 minutes: nothing high. Mazraoui and Zirkzee had gone off at 64 to 65 minutes. United's last seven regains all came in their own half, Sporting's passes in United's half rose from 58 before the break to 109 after it, and the equaliser came at 80 minutes.
04

How United won it

United went after Sporting's back three and wing-backs. Both central midfielders pushed into Sporting's half, and the back line held high behind them. Of the 24 times United won the ball in the final 40% of the pitch, 18 came straight off a Sporting centre-back or wing-back.

  • The wide players pressed the wing-backs. Amad made 14 defensive actions before the 64th minute, more than anyone, at an average spot inside Sporting's half on the right. Mbeumo did the same job on the left. Between them, Sporting's two wing-backs lost the ball to a high United regain 11 times.
  • The midfield pair pushed up. Mainoo and Tielemans made their defensive actions, on average, further forward than Fernandes. That shows they stepped onto Sporting's midfielders rather than sitting in front of the back four.
  • They won it by tackling, not by waiting. A third of the high regains (8 of 24) were tackles won, against a fifth of all regains. When United got close, they took the ball off a player rather than collecting a loose pass.
  • It came from organised pressing more than scrambling. Only 23 of the 63 regains (37%) came within five seconds of United losing the ball. That matches Chelsea away as the lowest share of the season, so most regains came from a set press on Sporting's build-up rather than a reaction to losing possession.
  • The back line squeezed up. Maguire and Mazraoui made their defensive actions, on average, about 45 metres from their own goal. That kept the team short, but it is also why Sporting kept trying balls in behind.

WhoScored lists United as a 4-2-3-1 throughout. Sporting started in a 3-1-4-2 and switched to a 3-4-2-1 at 71 minutes. The average positions above cover the first 64 minutes, before the substitutions changed the shape; the counts cover the whole match.

05

Why it bodes well

Sporting was the furthest up the pitch United have won the ball all season, by a long way, and it came in Pochettino's second game. I ran every United match this season through the same measures, and the gap is too big to call an ordinary night.

  • The change was where they pressed, not how hard. Sporting got about 13 passes in their build-up per United challenge. That is harder than the Carrick average of 16, but City at home (9) and Spurs at home (10) were tougher. United didn't run more; they pressed from a higher starting point. Positioning is coachable and repeatable in a way that one-off effort isn't.
  • It was organised. Most regains came from a set press on Sporting's build-up, not from scrambling after losing the ball (see How United won it). Set pressing patterns travel from game to game.
  • It came fast. Pochettino took over during the international break and had not finished a full week at the club when United played Sporting. A shape this clear after so few sessions suggests it was coached, not improvised.
  • The players for it are already there. Amad, Mainoo and Tielemans won the ball high most often, and all three are first-choice players.

The honest caveat is that this is one game. Anfield, his debut, was one of United's deepest games of the season (30 metres), and Sporting's back-three build-up invites a press more than most teams do. Brentford at home (28 November) and Newcastle away (2 December) will show whether the height holds.

06

What still needs fixing

The fix

Winning the ball high only pays if something happens next, and at Sporting very little did.

  • Too few shots from good regains. The 24 high regains led to a shot within 15 seconds twice (8%). Under Carrick, 30 of 155 high regains did (19%), so United won the ball in better places and did less with it.
  • The ball went straight back. After a high regain, United strung two or more passes together 10 times out of 24 and lost it again without completing a pass 11 times.
  • It stopped at 67 minutes. No high regains after that point, while Sporting moved to a 3-4-2-1 at 71 minutes and pushed United back. The equaliser came at 80. Whether that was legs or the changes at 64 to 65 minutes, a press that lasts two-thirds of a match won't hold leads.
  • A slow start. None of United's first seven regains came in Sporting's half; the press only switched on after 15 minutes.
